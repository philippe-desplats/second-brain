# Progressive disclosure in B2B search interfaces

> Captured from [Example Journal](https://example.com/articles/progressive-disclosure-b2b-search) on 2026-06-24. Personal working copy, kept for retrieval.

## Observations

- [quote] "Trade buyers do not browse, they verify: they arrive knowing the part number and leave if the first screen cannot confirm it."
- [quote] "Every filter you show before the first result is a question asked of someone who already knew the answer."
- [takeaway] Our catalog search should treat the part number as the primary path and faceted browsing as the fallback, which is the opposite of the build order we sketched at kickoff
- [takeaway] Worth testing the exact-reference path with the trade-buyer panel before committing the wireframes
- [question] Does this hold when the buyer knows the machine but not the part, which is the case Jane raised about maintenance teams

## Source

_Kept short here on purpose: a real clip carries the full article body under this heading, exactly as the Web Clipper captured it._

The article argues that B2B search and consumer search solve different problems. Consumer search helps someone discover what they want; professional search helps someone confirm what they already came for. Interfaces that borrow consumer patterns, filter panels first and results second, add friction for the buyer who arrived with a reference in hand.

The author reviews six industrial storefronts and finds that the three with the lowest abandonment all put a single reference field above everything else, deferring filters until a search returns more than one candidate.
